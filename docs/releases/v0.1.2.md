## Changed

- **"Who" is asked both ways.** The Who card answers with the person's role, what they are known for and their legacy line together. A new Who reverse card shows that description without the name and asks who it was; it replaces the two Legacy cards, whose single sentence was often too vague to answer.
- **Legacy is context, not a question.** A civilization's and a person's legacy line is shown on the backs of their cards; no card asks you to recall it. The deck is about 2,600 cards smaller for it.
- **Tiers for people, and an order for new cards.** People are tagged `TH::Tier::Essential` (about 150), `TH::Tier::Core` or `TH::Tier::Extended`, like civilizations. New cards come a tier at a time and in the order of history, each civilization followed by its people; the Essential tier is about 540 cards.
- **Links under every answer.** A row of small icons opens the Wikipedia article, a Google Images search and a YouTube search.
- **No Capital card that answers itself.** A civilization named after its only capital (the Republic of Genoa, Sparta, Carthage) has no Capital card; the capital is still shown on its other cards.
- **The deck is called Total History.** The deck, its note types and the file (`total_history.apkg`) take the new name, and its tags start with `TH::` instead of `PIH::`.
- **Legacy lines stand on their own.** A figure's legacy line no longer leans on the sentence before it ("Three months later …", "In this position …"), and only the person's own name is hidden: a line that names someone else inside their full name (Augustus for Tiberius), gives a nickname or another name, or names something after them (the Fibonacci numbers) is passed over for the next best sentence.
- **Figures are filed under the right state more often.** A generic title no longer makes someone a state's ruler (every Sultan of Delhi had been filed under the Beylik of Karaman), and citizenship no longer files French writers under Algeria or Austrians under each crown land.

## Fixed

- The United States is drawn in 1940, before the wartime occupations, and lists the Philippines overseas, no longer Greenland, Iceland and Palau. Land a state occupied overseas for five years or less is left off its map and its modern countries (Guam for the Empire of Japan, Corsica for Great Britain).
- Crimea counts as Ukraine everywhere, following recognised borders: on maps, in modern countries, in the regions of the Before and After cards and for people born there.
- Francoist Spain is followed by the Kingdom of Spain, not by the "Spanish transition to democracy".
- A figure's legacy line no longer opens on a group ("They embarked on …"), points at a time it leaves unnamed ("during this era"), or hides a namesake's name (Catherine the Great in Catherine I's line).
- A full review of every civilization and figure corrected wrong states (enemies and places of death), successions, modern countries, map years, roles, life dates, Known for lines and legacy lines, each correction citing its source.
- Where sources disagree, the card says so: the Sunda Kingdom began in 669 or 932.
